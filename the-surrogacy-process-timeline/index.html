The surrogacy process, stage by stage

There is no universal number of weeks or months for a surrogacy journey. Program workflow, matching, medical screening, legal requirements, fertility-clinic protocols and pregnancy all affect timing.

1

Initial application

You provide pregnancy history, health information, lifestyle details and preferences. The program uses this information for an initial eligibility review.

2

Records, medical and psychological review

Programs and fertility clinics may review prior obstetric records, medical history and other screening information. Psychological consultation is also common. Exact requirements vary by clinic and program.

3

Matching

A match requires mutual fit with intended parents. Compensation expectations, location, communication style, views on termination or multiples, travel, insurance and other preferences can affect how long matching takes.

4

Legal agreement

The surrogate and intended parents should have appropriate legal advice. The agreement addresses compensation and expenses, medical decision-making, communication, insurance and pregnancy-related contingencies. State law can affect what is required and enforceable.

5

Clinic clearance and cycle preparation

Once the clinic's required medical and legal clearances are complete, the clinic sets the medication and embryo-transfer protocol. Medication schedules and monitoring are clinic-specific.

6

Embryo transfer and pregnancy testing

The fertility clinic performs the transfer and determines the testing schedule. There is no single testing timeline that applies to every clinic or protocol.

7

Pregnancy and prenatal care

Care follows the fertility clinic and obstetric team's plan. Communication and intended-parent participation should follow the expectations agreed by everyone involved.

8

Delivery and legal/administrative steps

The parentage process depends on the governing state law and the facts of the case. Some jurisdictions use pre-birth procedures; others require post-birth steps. Do not assume legal parentage “transfers” in the same way everywhere.

What affects the timeline?

Delays can come from records collection, additional medical review, matching preferences, rematching, insurance questions, clinic scheduling, unsuccessful embryo transfer, legal complexity or pregnancy-related events. These variables make fixed promises misleading.

A good timeline explains the order of the process and the dependencies between stages; it does not promise that every surrogate will move through them on the same schedule.
