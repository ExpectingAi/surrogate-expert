The full surrogacy timeline, start to finish

Most journeys take 12–18 months from application to delivery. Here's exactly what happens at each stage.

See if you qualify
1

Application (Week 1–2)

You complete an initial application covering your health history, lifestyle, and preferences. This is the fastest step.

2

Medical & psychological screening (Weeks 2–6)

A fertility clinic evaluates your physical health; a licensed mental health professional confirms you're emotionally prepared.

3

Matching (4–12 weeks, varies widely)

You're presented with potential intended parent matches based on mutual fit. This stage's length depends heavily on your preferences and current demand.

4

Legal contracts (2–4 weeks)

You and the intended parents each retain independent attorneys, who negotiate and finalize the surrogacy agreement.

5

Medication & cycle prep (4–6 weeks)

Once contracts are signed, your clinic begins synchronizing your cycle in preparation for embryo transfer.

6

Embryo transfer & confirmation (2–3 weeks)

The transfer procedure itself takes minutes; pregnancy is confirmed via bloodwork roughly two weeks later.

7

Pregnancy (~40 weeks)

Standard prenatal care, with your intended parents involved to the extent you've both agreed.

8

Delivery & parentage transfer

At birth, legal parentage transfers to the intended parents per the pre-arranged court order — finalizing the journey.

What affects how long it takes

The biggest variable is matching — some surrogates are matched within weeks, others take a few months, largely depending on your specific preferences (location, relationship style, compensation expectations) and current demand from intended parents.

"There's no way to rush biology or the legal process safely — but every stage exists for a clear reason, and you'll always know what's coming next."
