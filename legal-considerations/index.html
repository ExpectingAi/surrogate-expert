How your rights are legally protected

Surrogacy contracts exist to protect you. Here's what's actually in them, and why independent legal counsel is essential.

See if you qualify

Legal note: This page is educational only and is not legal advice. Surrogacy law varies by state, county, court, family structure, genetics, and delivery location. Always speak with a licensed reproductive attorney before signing any agreement.

You get your own lawyer

Before any contract is signed, you should have independent legal counsel — a lawyer whose only job is to represent your interests, separate from the intended parents' attorney. In standard agency-supported journeys, the intended parents typically pay for your independent attorney.

What the surrogacy contract covers

Compensation & payment schedule

Exact amounts, payment timing, and what happens in scenarios such as twins, C-section, miscarriage, or cancelled cycles.

Medical decision-making

Your right to make decisions about your own body and how medical decisions are handled during the pregnancy.

Parentage process

How the intended parents' legal parentage will be established under the law of the delivery state.

Contact & communication

Agreed expectations for contact during and after the pregnancy.

Insurance & expenses

What is covered, by whom, and how reimbursements and uncovered costs are handled.

Contingency planning

What happens in rare but important scenarios, including selective reduction, pregnancy loss, or other complications.

Parentage orders: how legal parenthood is established

In a properly structured gestational surrogacy journey, parentage is handled through the contract and the court or statutory process in the delivery state. In many surrogacy-friendly states, intended parents can obtain a pre-birth parentage order. In other states, post-birth steps or additional procedures may be required.

Your attorney will explain exactly how this works in your state, including whether your name appears on any initial birth record, how the intended parents are recognized, and what documents are needed before and after delivery.

"The goal of the legal process is to protect you, the intended parents, and the baby — but the exact process depends on the delivery state and must be handled by qualified counsel."

State law matters

Surrogacy law varies significantly by state. Some states have explicit statutes, others rely on court practice or case law, and some restrict compensated surrogacy. See our state-by-state breakdown as a starting point, then confirm your specific situation with your independent attorney.

Common legal questions

Do I have to pay for my own lawyer?

Usually no. In standard arrangements, the intended parents cover the cost of your independent legal counsel.

Can I change my mind after signing the contract?

The contract is a binding legal agreement, so it is important to take the time you need before signing. Your attorney should walk through every clause with you before you agree.

What if the intended parents don't pay as agreed?

Your contract should include specific payment terms, escrow requirements, and remedies if those terms are not met. This is exactly why independent counsel and a properly funded escrow matter.
