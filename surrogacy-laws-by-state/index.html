Surrogacy laws by state

A state-by-state starting point for women considering becoming a gestational surrogate. State law can affect agreements, eligibility, parentage procedure, compensation rules and delivery planning.

Legal note: This page is educational only and is not legal advice. Surrogacy law changes over time, and the result can depend on the statute, the agreement, the parties, residence, medical procedure, expected delivery location and other facts. Confirm your specific situation with licensed reproductive counsel.

Why this varies by state

The United States does not have one comprehensive federal surrogacy code. Some states have detailed statutes governing gestational agreements. Others address only selected issues, rely partly on parentage law or case law, or impose restrictions on particular arrangements.

For that reason, this site does not label states as “friendly,” “conditional,” “moderate,” or “restrictive.” Those labels can hide important statutory conditions and can become inaccurate when the law changes.

Use the state pages to identify the legal questions that matter. Do not use a state ranking as a substitute for legal advice about your own journey.
