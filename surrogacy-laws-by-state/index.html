Surrogacy laws by state

A practical overview for women considering becoming a gestational surrogate. State law can affect contracts, parentage, delivery planning, and what legal protections are available.

Legal note: This page is educational only and is not legal advice. Surrogacy law changes and can vary by state, county, judge, family structure, genetic connection, and delivery hospital. Always confirm your plan with a licensed reproductive attorney in the delivery state.

Why this varies so much by state

Surrogacy law in the U.S. is set state by state, not federally. Whether compensated surrogacy is allowed, whether intended parents can get a pre-birth order, and how smoothly the process runs can look different depending on where you live or where you deliver.

Most states fall into three broad categories: surrogacy-friendly, conditional / case-by-case, and restrictive. These labels are starting points, not legal conclusions for your individual situation.

"Laws change — sometimes quickly. Use this page as a starting point, then confirm your exact situation with a reproductive attorney licensed in the delivery state."
