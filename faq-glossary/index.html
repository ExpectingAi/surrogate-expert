Surrogacy questions and terms

Quick answers to common questions, plus plain-language definitions of terms you are likely to encounter during a gestational-surrogacy journey.

Frequently asked

Common questions

In gestational surrogacy, the gestational carrier does not provide the egg, so she is not genetically related to the embryo through her own egg. The embryo may be created using intended-parent or donor gametes.
There is no universal timeline. Screening, matching, legal work, clinic clearance, transfer attempts and pregnancy can each vary substantially. Treat fixed total-duration estimates as program-specific planning ranges, not guarantees.
Insurance is reviewed case by case. An existing policy may or may not cover a surrogate pregnancy, and some journeys require other coverage or a separate plan. Review exclusions, deductibles and uncovered costs before treatment.
Matching practices vary by program, but mutual agreement is a common model. Ask how profiles are presented, whether either side can decline a proposed match, and what happens if preferences do not align.
The fertility clinic should explain the medical reason for its decision. Some concerns may be temporary or require further evaluation; others may make pregnancy inappropriate. A medical-clearance decision should be individualized rather than treated as a generic program rule.
Communication and appointment involvement should be discussed before matching and reflected in expectations or the agreement where appropriate. The surrogate remains the patient and retains authority over her own medical care.

Glossary of terms

Gestational carrier / gestational surrogate

A person who carries a pregnancy for intended parent or parents and does not provide the egg used to create the embryo.

Intended parent(s)

The person or people who intend to become the child's legal parent or parents. The legal route depends on the jurisdiction and facts of the arrangement.

IVF (in vitro fertilization)

A process in which eggs are fertilized outside the body to create embryos, one of which may later be transferred to the gestational carrier.

Embryo transfer

A medical procedure in which an embryo is placed into the uterus. The exact protocol and medications are determined by the fertility clinic.

Parentage order

A court order addressing legal parentage. Whether an order is available before or after birth, and whether a court order is required at all, depends on state law and the facts of the case.

Independent legal counsel

An attorney representing one party's own interests rather than both sides jointly. Whether separate counsel is legally required depends on the applicable jurisdiction and statutory framework, though it is common in gestational-surrogacy practice.

Escrow account

An account or payment arrangement used to hold and disburse funds according to agreed instructions. The structure and regulatory requirements vary by program and jurisdiction.

Base compensation

The core agreed compensation amount, separate from reimbursements, allowances or event-specific payments where those are included.

Matching

The process by which a potential surrogate and intended parent or parents evaluate whether they want to proceed together.
