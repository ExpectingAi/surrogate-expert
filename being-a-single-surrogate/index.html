Can you be a surrogate if you are single?

Being single is not, by itself, a universal medical disqualifier for gestational surrogacy. The practical question is whether the clinic, program and applicable state law allow the proposed arrangement and whether you have enough support for the journey.

Relationship status and eligibility

Professional medical guidance focuses on health, pregnancy history, informed consent, psychosocial readiness and support rather than imposing one nationwide marital-status rule. Individual programs may still use their own administrative criteria, and state law can create additional requirements in some arrangements.

Why support still matters

A reliable support system can be important during fertility treatment, pregnancy, delivery and postpartum recovery. That support might come from a partner, relative, friend or another trusted person. The exact expectations differ by program and by individual circumstances.

Medical screening

The fertility clinic evaluates the same core medical issues regardless of whether a candidate is single or partnered: pregnancy history, current health, medications, infectious-disease screening and whether another pregnancy is medically appropriate.

Psychosocial evaluation

Programs commonly assess practical support, stressors, informed consent, expectations and emotional readiness. Being single does not answer those questions automatically in either direction.

Legal agreement

The contract and parentage plan should be reviewed under the law that applies to the specific journey. If a candidate is married or partnered, a spouse or partner may have legal relevance in some jurisdictions or programs; if single, different documentation may apply. State-specific counsel should confirm the details.

Compensation

Relationship status should not be assumed to determine compensation. Compensation depends on the agreement and program structure. Compare the actual written terms rather than relying on a general rule about single or partnered carriers.

Questions to ask a program

  • Does the program have any relationship-status requirement?
  • What support-person expectations apply?
  • Does state law create any spouse or partner documentation requirement?
  • How are emergency childcare, travel and postpartum support handled?
  • Will the fertility clinic make a separate medical-clearance decision?

Single status is one fact about a candidate, not a substitute for medical, psychosocial, program and legal review.
