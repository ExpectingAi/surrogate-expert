Yes — single women can be surrogates

No spouse or partner required. Here's what's genuinely different, and what support looks like.

See if you qualify

There's no marriage requirement

A surprising number of women assume they need a spouse or partner to become a surrogate — that's not true. Single women make up a meaningful portion of surrogates, and the core requirements (age, prior pregnancy, health, stability) apply the same way regardless of relationship status.

What's genuinely different

Support person

You'll want to identify someone — a friend, family member, or another surrogate — to be your support person, since this role is usually filled by a spouse.

Practical logistics

Things like transportation to appointments may need more advance planning without a partner's built-in flexibility.

Emotional support network

Worth being intentional about who you lean on through the highs and lows of the journey.

What's exactly the same

Medical screening, compensation, legal protections, and the matching process all work identically regardless of your relationship status. Intended parents matching with you see your full profile and circumstances — being single is simply one detail among many, not a barrier.

"Plenty of intended parents specifically appreciate working with single surrogates — it's never been a dealbreaker, just one part of who you are."

Building your support system

If you don't have an obvious support person, raise this with your case coordinator early — many programs can help connect you with other surrogates or additional resources to make sure you're not navigating the journey alone.
