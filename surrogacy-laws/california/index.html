Surrogacy laws in California

California has some of the clearest, most established surrogacy law in the country — a major reason many surrogacy journeys happen here.

See if you qualify

Legal note: This page is educational only and is not legal advice. Always consult a California reproductive attorney about your specific contract, medical timing, insurance, and parentage process.

Is compensated surrogacy legal in California?

Yes. California has an explicit statutory framework that recognizes and regulates gestational surrogacy arrangements, including compensation to the surrogate when the statutory requirements are followed.

Are pre-birth parentage orders available?

Yes. California courts routinely issue parentage orders confirming the intended parents as the legal parents. The exact process, timing, and birth certificate procedures should be handled by California counsel.

What the law requires

Written agreement

A surrogacy contract meeting statutory requirements, signed before embryo transfer or injectable medication for embryo transfer begins.

Independent legal counsel

Separate attorneys for the surrogate and intended parents.

Medical and insurance review

Medical readiness, insurance coverage, reimbursement responsibilities, and possible uncovered costs should be reviewed before the agreement is finalized.

Clear parentage plan

The agreement and court process establish how the intended parents will be recognized as the legal parents.

"California's statute doesn't just permit surrogacy — it sets out a structured process, which is part of why intended parents and surrogates often prefer it."

What this means for you as a surrogate

If you live in California, or plan to deliver here, you are in one of the most legally established environments for surrogacy. Your specific contract, insurance plan, medical timing, and parentage order still matter, so your independent attorney should walk you through the details before you sign anything.
