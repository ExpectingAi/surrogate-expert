Surrogacy laws in Nebraska

Nebraska law states that surrogacy contracts are void and unenforceable, making it one of the most restrictive states in the country.

See if you qualify

Is compensated surrogacy legal in Nebraska?

No, in the traditional contract sense. Nebraska statute explicitly states that surrogate parenthood contracts are void as against public policy, which sets it apart from the vast majority of other states.

What does this mean in practice?

Because surrogacy contracts aren't enforceable in Nebraska, intended parents and surrogates working with Nebraska residents typically structure their journey to take place in a different, more supportive state — including for the embryo transfer, pregnancy, and delivery.

What this means in practice

No contract enforcement

Even with a signed agreement, Nebraska courts won't enforce it as a surrogacy contract.

Out-of-state arrangements common

Many Nebraska residents pursuing surrogacy structure their journey around a different state's laws.

Specialized attorney essential

A reproductive attorney can help you understand exactly what your real options look like.

Not the same as banning the practice

Surrogacy itself isn't illegal in Nebraska — it's specifically the contract's enforceability that's restricted.

"Nebraska's restriction is specifically about contract enforceability — it doesn't mean surrogacy is impossible for Nebraska residents, just that the legal structure needs to look different."

What this means for you as a surrogate

If you're located in Nebraska and interested in surrogacy, it's worth having an early, detailed conversation with a reproductive attorney about your real options — including the common path of pursuing the legal and medical process in a neighboring state with clearer, more supportive law.
