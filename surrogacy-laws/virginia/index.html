Surrogacy laws in Virginia

Virginia has a surrogacy statute requiring court approval of the agreement before the embryo transfer, with specific eligibility requirements for intended parents.

See if you qualify

Is compensated surrogacy legal in Virginia?

Yes, with structure. Virginia's surrogacy statute authorizes compensated gestational surrogacy, but requires the agreement to be approved by a court before the embryo transfer — a more involved process than some other states.

Are pre-birth parentage orders available?

Yes, as part of the statute's court-approval process, when all requirements are met.

What the law requires

Court approval before transfer

Virginia requires the surrogacy agreement to be approved by a court before medical procedures begin.

Written agreement

A surrogacy contract meeting the statute's detailed requirements.

Independent legal counsel

Separate attorneys for the surrogate and intended parents.

Medical & mental health evaluation

Both are statutory requirements in Virginia.

"Virginia's upfront court approval process adds a step compared to some other states, but it also means everyone has clarity and court backing before medical procedures begin."

What this means for you as a surrogate

Virginia offers a structured, statute-based process. Because of the upfront court approval requirement, timelines may run slightly longer than in some other states — your independent attorney will walk you through exactly what to expect.
