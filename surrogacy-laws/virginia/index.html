Surrogacy laws in Virginia

Virginia has statutory provisions addressing surrogacy, but the legal route is narrower and more procedure-dependent than a broad modern gestational-surrogacy code. Yellow reflects that limitation, not a judgment that surrogacy is prohibited.

Legal note: Educational information only. Virginia's statutory requirements, court procedure, compensation rules and parentage consequences should be reviewed with Virginia-licensed reproductive counsel before an agreement or treatment.

Why Virginia is yellow

Virginia law addresses assisted conception and surrogacy through a statutory scheme with specific conditions and court procedures. Whether a proposed arrangement fits the available statutory route depends on the facts, so the state is better described as limited or fact-dependent than simply “friendly” or “restrictive.”

Court and agreement requirements

Virginia law includes formal requirements for surrogacy arrangements and court involvement in certain pathways. The timing and required approvals should be confirmed for the specific agreement before medical treatment proceeds.

Compensation and expenses

Virginia law places limits on what payments may be made in connection with a surrogacy arrangement. Do not assume that compensation structures commonly used in other states are permitted in Virginia; counsel should review the exact terms.

Parentage

Parentage procedure depends on which statutory route applies and whether its requirements are satisfied. Generic claims about automatic pre-birth orders or guaranteed birth-certificate outcomes are not appropriate.

What to verify

  • Which Virginia statutory pathway applies to the proposed arrangement.
  • Whether any court approval is required and when.
  • What compensation and expense payments are permitted.
  • How parentage will be established.
  • How the surrogate's independent counsel and medical autonomy are protected.
