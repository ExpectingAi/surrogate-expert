Surrogacy laws in Mississippi

Mississippi does not currently have a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute creating one uniform statewide pathway for agreements and parentage.

What the yellow status means: Mississippi's legal path depends more heavily on general parentage law, court procedure and the facts of the specific arrangement than in states with a dedicated surrogacy code.

What the legal framework looks like

Because Mississippi lacks a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute, parties should not assume that eligibility rules, contract formalities or parentage procedures used in another state automatically apply here.

Parentage procedure

The availability, timing and form of any parentage order should be confirmed for the specific case with Mississippi reproductive counsel. A gestational agreement should not be treated as a guarantee of a pre-birth order, birth-certificate result or other particular court outcome.

Agreement and compensation

The proposed agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical autonomy, legal costs, termination provisions and dispute procedures. The enforceability and treatment of those provisions should be reviewed under current Mississippi law.

What this means for a surrogate

Before signing an agreement or beginning treatment, use independent Mississippi reproductive counsel to confirm the governing law, jurisdiction, parentage strategy and contract terms for the proposed match.
