Surrogacy laws in Alaska

Alaska does not have a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute creating one uniform statewide pathway for agreements and parentage.

What the yellow status means: the legal pathway is more dependent on existing parentage law, court procedure and the facts of the arrangement than in states with a dedicated surrogacy code.

What the legal framework looks like

Because Alaska does not provide a comprehensive statutory gestational-surrogacy framework, parties should not assume that contract formalities, eligibility rules or parentage procedures used in another state automatically apply in Alaska.

Parentage procedure

The availability, timing and form of a parentage order should be confirmed for the specific case with Alaska reproductive counsel. This page does not treat a pre-birth order or a particular birth-record result as automatic.

Agreement and compensation

The proposed agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical autonomy, legal costs, termination provisions and dispute procedures. The enforceability and treatment of those provisions should be reviewed under current Alaska law.

What this means for a surrogate

Before signing an agreement or beginning treatment, use independent Alaska counsel to confirm the governing law, jurisdiction, parentage strategy and contract terms for the proposed match.
