Surrogacy laws in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania does not currently have a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute creating one uniform statewide pathway for agreements and parentage.

What the yellow status means: the framework depends more heavily on existing parentage law, court procedure and the facts of the arrangement than in states with a dedicated surrogacy code.

What the legal framework looks like

Because Pennsylvania lacks a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute, parties should not assume that contract formalities, eligibility rules or parentage procedures used elsewhere automatically apply statewide.

Parentage procedure

The availability, timing and form of a parentage order should be confirmed for the specific case with Pennsylvania reproductive counsel. Court procedure may vary, but this page does not rank counties or imply that one county guarantees a particular result.

Agreement and compensation

The agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical autonomy, legal costs, termination provisions and dispute procedures. Enforceability and treatment of those provisions should be reviewed under current Pennsylvania law.

Pending legislation

Proposed legislation can change the legal landscape, but a bill should not be treated as current law unless it has been enacted and taken effect.

What this means for a surrogate

Before signing an agreement or beginning treatment, use independent Pennsylvania reproductive counsel to confirm the governing law, jurisdiction, parentage strategy and contract terms for the proposed match.
