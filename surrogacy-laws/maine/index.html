Surrogacy laws in Maine

Maine has statutory provisions governing gestational surrogacy agreements and parentage. The green classification describes the existence of a comprehensive statutory framework; it is not a recommendation or guarantee for any individual arrangement.

Legal note: This page is educational only. Eligibility, agreement requirements, compensation, jurisdiction and parentage procedure should be confirmed with Maine-licensed reproductive counsel.

What Maine law covers

Maine's parentage law contains provisions addressing gestational surrogacy agreements, including requirements for the parties and the agreement and a statutory route for establishing parentage when applicable conditions are satisfied.

Compensation

Maine law permits gestational-surrogacy agreements that may include compensation, subject to the governing statute and the terms of the agreement. The legal effect of a specific compensation arrangement should be reviewed by counsel.

Parentage procedure

Maine provides a statutory parentage process for qualifying surrogacy arrangements. The exact filing, timing and vital-record steps depend on the facts and should not be treated as an automatic pre-birth outcome.

What to verify before signing

  • Whether the agreement satisfies Maine's statutory requirements.
  • Whether each party has the legal advice required for the arrangement.
  • How compensation, expenses and insurance obligations are documented.
  • Which court has jurisdiction and what parentage procedure will be used.
  • How medical decision-making and the surrogate's autonomy are protected.

What this means for a prospective surrogate

The existence of a detailed statute can make the legal questions easier to identify, but it does not eliminate case-specific requirements. Have your own attorney review the agreement and current law before treatment begins.
