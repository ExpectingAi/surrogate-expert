Surrogacy laws in New Jersey

New Jersey's Gestational Carrier Agreement Act, enacted in 2018, provides a statutory framework for qualifying gestational-carrier agreements and parentage. Green describes the framework, not a guaranteed outcome.

Legal note: This page is educational only. New Jersey counsel should confirm eligibility, agreement formalities, parentage procedure and any later statutory changes that apply to the specific journey.

What the 2018 law changed

The Gestational Carrier Agreement Act created an express statutory pathway for gestational-carrier agreements after earlier New Jersey law had treated compensated traditional-surrogacy arrangements differently. The current analysis should focus on the statute and the facts of the gestational-carrier arrangement rather than broad labels about the state.

Agreement and compensation

The statute sets requirements for qualifying agreements and permits compensation within the statutory framework. The agreement should address compensation, expenses, insurance and the parties' responsibilities with independent legal review.

Parentage

New Jersey law provides a statutory route for intended-parent parentage in qualifying arrangements. Court timing and vital-record procedures should be confirmed for the actual case rather than treated as automatic.

What to verify

  • Whether the parties and agreement satisfy the current statute.
  • Timing of execution relative to medical procedures.
  • Independent legal representation and informed consent.
  • Insurance and financial obligations.
  • The planned parentage filing and jurisdiction.
