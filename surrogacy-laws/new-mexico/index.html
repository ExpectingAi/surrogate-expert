Surrogacy laws in New Mexico

New Mexico's parentage law has included gestational-agreement concepts, while later bills have proposed broader and more detailed surrogacy provisions. Current applicability should be verified against the law in force for the specific case.

What the yellow status means: this page does not treat proposed legislation as enacted law and does not assume that a particular parentage procedure is available in every case.

Current law versus proposed changes

New Mexico has addressed gestational agreements within its parentage-law framework. More recent bills have proposed detailed eligibility, agreement, medical-autonomy and parentage provisions. A bill should not be treated as current law unless it has been enacted and taken effect.

Parentage procedure

The availability, timing and form of a parentage order should be confirmed for the specific case with New Mexico reproductive counsel. Do not assume that a pre-birth order or birth-record outcome is automatic.

Agreement and medical autonomy

The agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical autonomy, legal costs, termination provisions and dispute procedures. The surrogate remains the patient and retains decision-making authority over her own medical care.

What this means for a surrogate

Before signing an agreement or beginning treatment, use independent New Mexico reproductive counsel to confirm which statutory provisions are currently in force and how they apply to the proposed match.
