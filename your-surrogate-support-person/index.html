Support during a surrogacy journey

A gestational-surrogacy journey can involve medical appointments, travel, pregnancy recovery, family logistics and emotionally demanding decisions. Having reliable support can make those practical demands easier to manage.

What a support person may help with

A support person may be a spouse, partner, relative, close friend or another trusted person. Depending on your circumstances, they may help with transportation, childcare, recovery after procedures, scheduling, emotional support or simply being available when plans change.

Is a formal support person always required?

No single rule applies to every clinic or surrogacy program. Some programs place significant emphasis on a surrogate candidate's support system, while others assess practical readiness in different ways. Ask the specific clinic or program what it expects and whether a named support person must participate in screening or appointments.

What if you are single?

Relationship status is not the same thing as having or lacking support. A single surrogate candidate may have a strong network of family and friends, while a partnered candidate may still have limited practical support. Programs should evaluate the actual support available rather than rely only on marital or relationship status.

Medical and legal boundaries

A support person does not replace your own medical consent or legal advice. You remain the patient for your medical care, and any legal rights or obligations should be explained by qualified counsel for the specific arrangement.

Questions to ask before proceeding

  • Does the clinic or program expect a named support person?
  • Will that person need to attend screening, transfer or other appointments?
  • What practical help would you need if you have activity restrictions or a complication?
  • Who can help with childcare, transportation or overnight support if needed?
  • What counseling or peer-support resources are available through the program?

A strong support plan is about practical readiness and informed choice, not about needing someone else's permission to participate.
