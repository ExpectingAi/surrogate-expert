Risks to understand before becoming a surrogate

Gestational surrogacy involves the risks of pregnancy plus legal, emotional, insurance, and financial issues that should be addressed before treatment begins.

Use the eligibility self-check

Medical/legal note: This page is educational only and is not medical or legal advice. Individual risk, insurance coverage, contract terms, and parentage law vary by person and jurisdiction.

Medical risks

A gestational-surrogacy pregnancy can involve the same types of complications as other pregnancies, including hypertensive disorders, gestational diabetes, bleeding, infection, pregnancy loss, and cesarean delivery. Medical screening is intended to assess suitability and identify known concerns, but it cannot eliminate pregnancy risk or predict every complication.

Embryo transfer may not result in pregnancy

Not every embryo transfer results in an ongoing pregnancy. What happens after an unsuccessful transfer depends on medical advice, embryo availability, the agreement between the parties, and whether everyone chooses to continue.

Emotional and relationship risk

Surrogacy can involve complex expectations among the carrier, intended parents, partners, children, and extended family. Psychosocial evaluation and counseling can help the parties discuss communication, boundaries, difficult pregnancy decisions, and expectations before moving forward.

Legal risk

Surrogacy law varies by state. Contract enforceability, eligibility, parentage procedures, and the timing of court orders are jurisdiction-specific. Appropriate legal advice for the carrier and intended parents is important before treatment proceeds.

Insurance and financial risk

Health-insurance coverage for a surrogate pregnancy cannot be assumed from the existence of a policy. Coverage, exclusions, deductibles, liens, and reimbursement obligations should be reviewed before treatment. Payment-related risk can also be reduced through clear contract terms and an appropriately funded escrow or other agreed payment-management structure.

Common safeguards

  • Medical and psychosocial evaluation before treatment
  • Appropriate independent legal advice where required or advisable
  • Insurance review before treatment
  • Written compensation and reimbursement terms
  • Escrow or another agreed payment-management structure
  • A clear plan for communication, medical decisions, travel, and delivery

These steps help manage risk; they do not make the journey risk-free.
