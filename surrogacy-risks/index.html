An honest look at the risks

No guide does its job if it only tells you the good parts. Here's a realistic look at what could go differently than planned — and how each risk is managed.

See if you qualify

Medical/legal note: This page is educational only and is not medical or legal advice. Pregnancy risks, insurance coverage, and legal protections vary by person, clinic, contract, state, and delivery location. Always consult qualified professionals about your specific situation.

Medical risks

Gestational surrogacy carries pregnancy risks, including rare but serious complications such as preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, bleeding, infection, or the need for a C-section. Screening exists to identify anyone for whom these risks are elevated before the journey begins.

The embryo transfer might not work the first time

Not every transfer results in pregnancy. If this happens, it is not a reflection of anything you did. Your clinic will review the cycle, and another transfer attempt may be possible if more embryos are available and everyone agrees to continue.

Emotional risk

Surrogacy is a significant emotional commitment. Most surrogates describe the experience positively, but it is real work — managing your own feelings, your family's reactions, and building a relationship with people you may not have known before. Psychological screening and ongoing support exist to help you navigate this well.

"The honest answer is: many journeys go well, but a good program prepares you for both smooth moments and hard ones."

Legal risk

Surrogacy law varies by state, and a poorly written contract or unclear parentage process can create real complications. This is why independent legal counsel, a clear contract, and a state-specific legal plan matter so much.

Financial risk

If an intended parent were to default on payment, you could be left in a difficult position. Escrow accounts, clear payment schedules, and a properly funded contract help reduce that risk.

How these risks are minimized

  • Thorough medical and psychological screening before final clearance
  • Independent legal representation, typically paid for by the intended parents
  • An insurance and reimbursement plan reviewed before contracts are signed
  • Compensation held in escrow rather than paid directly by the intended parents
  • Ongoing case management and support throughout the journey

None of this eliminates risk entirely. But a well-structured program addresses each area directly instead of leaving you to navigate it alone.
