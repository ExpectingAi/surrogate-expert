What do U.S. surrogacy programs publicly advertise in 2026?

Public compensation pages can help you compare offers, but they do not establish a single U.S. market rate. This page separates verified program examples from claims about what completed matches actually close at.

Short answer: One major U.S. program, American Surrogacy, currently publishes first-time base compensation of $55,000–$65,000+ and experienced-surrogate base compensation of $70,000–$90,000+. It publishes total packages of $70,000–$80,000+ for first-time surrogates and $90,000–$110,000+ for experienced surrogates. These are that program’s advertised ranges — not a verified national market-clearing rate.

Why this is not a national “market rate”

Published compensation pages are recruitment and program-pricing materials. They tell you what a provider chooses to advertise, but they do not reveal the full distribution of completed matches across the United States.

There is another important bias: currently available surrogate inventories can overrepresent candidates who remain unmatched longer. Higher compensation expectations or narrower matching preferences can contribute to longer visibility, so an available-profile catalog should not be treated as a representative transaction sample.

Base pay and total package are different

  • Base compensation is the core payment for carrying the pregnancy.
  • Total package may add allowances, milestone payments, insurance-related benefits and other contract-specific amounts.
  • Reimbursements repay expenses and should not automatically be treated as earnings when comparing offers.

How to compare offers accurately

  1. Compare base compensation with base compensation.
  2. Separate guaranteed payments from contingent payments.
  3. Separate reimbursements from compensation.
  4. Identify what changes for C-section, multiples, bed rest, cancelled cycles or failed transfers.
  5. Review payment timing and escrow terms.
  6. Have independent reproductive counsel review the final agreement.

What would constitute a stronger market benchmark?

A more defensible benchmark would use anonymized completed matches, not current inventory. Useful reporting would include median compensation, 25th and 75th percentiles, state, prior surrogate experience, sample size, and a defined time period. Until that dataset is available, this site will label provider pricing as advertised or published figures rather than market-clearing rates.
