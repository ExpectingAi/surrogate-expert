How insurance works during your surrogacy journey

Your medical care is fully covered — here’s exactly how insurance works in 2026, what a dedicated policy costs, and what to know if you’re in California.

See if you qualify
✓ Updated June 2026 — surrogacy-specific insurance premiums have roughly doubled since 2021. California surrogates should be aware of the lien risk (SB 257 was vetoed in Oct 2025).

The bottom line: you are never financially responsible for your own medical costs

All pregnancy-related medical expenses during your surrogacy journey — prenatal care, delivery, complications, postpartum recovery — are the intended parents’ financial responsibility. This is documented in your legal contract before you begin. You will never receive a medical bill for costs related to the surrogate pregnancy.

Why a separate policy is usually needed

Many personal health insurance plans explicitly exclude surrogate pregnancies. Because of this, a dedicated surrogacy insurance policy is typically arranged and paid for by the intended parents before the medical process begins. There are two scenarios:

✅ Best case: Your existing ACA plan has no surrogacy exclusion

If your personal health insurance is an ACA-compliant plan without a surrogacy exclusion, it may be usable for the pregnancy. The intended parents cover your deductibles and out-of-pocket costs. This is verified by a specialist before matching — you don’t need to figure this out yourself.

⚠️ Standard case: A dedicated policy is purchased

If your existing plan excludes surrogacy (common with employer self-funded/ERISA plans) or you’re uninsured, the intended parents purchase a surrogacy-specific policy. In 2026, these policies typically cost $15,000–$35,000 for the full journey — entirely at the intended parents’ expense, not yours.

What’s covered

Prenatal care

Regular checkups, screenings, blood work, and standard pregnancy monitoring throughout.

Delivery & hospital stay

Labor, delivery, and your full hospital stay through discharge.

Pregnancy complications

Coverage for complications including preeclampsia, preterm labor, bed rest, or C-section.

Postpartum recovery

Follow-up care directly related to the pregnancy and delivery.

What about ERISA / employer plans?

Employer self-funded health plans (governed by federal ERISA law rather than state ACA rules) can exclude surrogacy more easily and are not subject to the same state insurance regulations. If your health insurance comes from an employer self-funded plan, it is more likely to have a surrogacy exclusion. A fertility insurance specialist reviews your specific policy — this happens before matching, not after.

California surrogates: the lien risk

If you are a California surrogate with existing health insurance, be aware that some insurers may attempt to place a lien on your compensation to recover costs paid for the surrogate pregnancy. Legislation to ban this practice (California SB 257) was vetoed by the Governor in October 2025, so the lien risk remains active. Reputable agencies working with California surrogates have processes to assess and mitigate this risk — ask specifically about it before proceeding.

“You should never be financially exposed for medical costs related to this pregnancy — that’s the whole point of a dedicated policy. In 2026, intended parents budget $15,000–$35,000 for this line item specifically so you don’t have to worry about it.”

Questions worth asking your agency

  • Will a specialist verify my existing policy before I begin medications?
  • If a dedicated policy is needed, when does it go into effect?
  • Who handles insurance claims and paperwork — me, or the agency?
  • If I’m in California, has the lien risk for my policy been assessed?
  • What is the out-of-pocket maximum on the policy, and who covers it?
