Matching

Why do some surrogates take longer to match?

A longer wait usually means the pool of compatible intended parents is smaller—not that the surrogate is necessarily a weaker candidate.

The most common reasons

  • Higher compensation expectations: fewer intended parents may have the budget.
  • Narrow matching preferences: restrictions on location, family type, communication or other journey preferences reduce possible matches.
  • Insurance uncertainty: intended parents may hesitate if coverage is unclear or expensive.
  • State and travel logistics: legal, clinic and delivery considerations can narrow fit.
  • Medical-history questions: additional review can slow the process even when a candidate may ultimately be approved.
  • Responsiveness: slow records collection or communication can cause promising matches to stall.

Why available-profile lists can create the wrong impression

Visible inventory is affected by how long profiles remain unmatched. Surrogates who are easy to match may leave a catalog quickly, while profiles with higher compensation or narrower preferences may remain visible longer. That means the average of “currently available” profiles can overstate the compensation level or restrictions typical of completed matches.

Does waiting longer mean you should lower compensation?

Not automatically. Compensation is only one factor. Before changing it, look at whether your profile is complete, whether your preferences are unusually narrow, whether insurance is clear, and whether records are ready for review. If compensation is well above comparable advertised programs, however, it may be reducing the number of intended parents who can consider the match.

What can help?

Ask for specific feedback: Is the limiting issue budget, medical clearance, geography, insurance, or preferences? A precise answer is more useful than simply hearing that a match “takes time.”
