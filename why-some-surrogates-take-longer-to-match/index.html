Matching

Why can some surrogates take longer to match?

A longer wait can reflect a smaller pool of compatible intended parents, additional screening or logistics, or simply the timing of available matches. It does not by itself establish that a surrogate is a weaker candidate.

Factors that can lengthen matching

  • Compensation expectations: higher requested compensation may reduce the number of intended parents who can consider a match.
  • Matching preferences: preferences about geography, family type, communication and other journey issues affect compatibility.
  • Insurance: unclear coverage or the need for additional insurance review can add time.
  • State and travel logistics: legal, clinic and delivery considerations can affect fit.
  • Medical-history review: additional records or specialist review can extend screening.
  • Records and communication: delays in obtaining records or responding can slow a potential match.

Why available-profile lists can create the wrong impression

Visible inventory is affected by how long profiles remain unmatched. Profiles that match quickly leave a catalog sooner, while profiles with higher compensation expectations or narrower compatibility may remain visible longer. That creates survivorship bias, so a snapshot of currently available profiles should not be treated as a clean sample of completed-match compensation or preferences.

Does waiting longer mean you should lower compensation?

Not automatically. Compensation is only one factor. It is more useful to identify whether the limiting issue is budget, medical review, geography, insurance, records, or matching preferences. If requested compensation is materially above comparable published program ranges, it may reduce the number of intended parents who can consider the match, but that is not the only possible explanation.

What can help?

Ask for specific feedback about which factor is limiting compatibility. A concrete explanation is more useful than assuming that time alone indicates a problem.
