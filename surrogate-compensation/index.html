How surrogate compensation works

Compensation is more than one headline number. Your agreement should clearly separate base compensation, allowances, reimbursements, insurance-related costs and payments triggered by specific events.

Base compensation

Base compensation is the core agreed payment for the journey. The amount can vary by program, experience, location and the facts of the match. It should not be treated as a universal market rate or inferred from a database of currently available profiles.

Allowances and reimbursements

Your agreement may provide for journey-related expenses such as travel, mileage, childcare, lost wages, maternity clothing, medically required support or other documented costs. The categories, limits and documentation requirements differ by contract.

Event-specific payments

Some contracts include additional payments for defined events such as embryo transfer, invasive procedures, multiple pregnancy, caesarean delivery, cancelled cycles or other circumstances. There is no universal schedule that applies to every program.

Insurance and medical costs

Insurance should be reviewed before treatment. The parties should understand whether an existing policy can be used, whether other coverage is needed and how deductibles, exclusions and uncovered pregnancy-related expenses will be handled.

How payments are administered

Many journeys use an escrow account or another third-party payment process. Funding, release conditions and documentation should be set out before the journey reaches payment milestones.

What should be clear before you sign?

  • What amount is base compensation
  • Which payments are guaranteed and which are conditional
  • Which expenses are reimbursed at cost
  • What documentation is required
  • How insurance-related costs are handled
  • How and when funds are released
  • What happens if a cycle is cancelled, a pregnancy ends early or rematching is required

Base pay versus total package

Do not compare one program's “base compensation” with another program's “total package” as though they are the same metric. Ask for an itemized schedule and compare equivalent categories.

The contract's itemized compensation schedule matters more than a headline number.

Tax questions

Tax treatment can be fact-specific. Do not assume compensation is taxable or tax-free based on a generic article; consult a qualified tax professional familiar with the circumstances of your agreement.
