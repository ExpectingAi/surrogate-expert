Do you qualify to be a surrogate?

A clear list of common requirements, plus what varies by agency, clinic, and state.

Check your eligibility

Important: Requirements vary by agency, fertility clinic, intended-parent program, insurance situation, and state law. This page is educational only and does not determine final eligibility.

Core requirements

Usually age 21–40

Many agencies prefer 21–40 or 21–42, while some clinics may consider candidates up to 45 with careful risk review.

At least one prior birth

You will generally need to have given birth before, ideally with no major complications during pregnancy or delivery.

Stable family environment

Programs look for a safe, supportive home environment and reliable support during appointments, pregnancy, and recovery.

Healthy BMI

Many programs use a BMI range around 18–32, though exact limits vary by clinic and medical history.

U.S. citizen or permanent resident

Required by most U.S.-based programs for legal, insurance, and practical reasons.

Non-smoker, drug-free

No nicotine, recreational drugs, or unsafe substance use. This is usually verified through screening.

Lifestyle & stability requirements

Beyond the medical basics, programs look for stability: reliable transportation, ability to attend appointments, financial independence, and a support system. Government assistance does not always automatically disqualify someone, but some programs review it carefully to avoid financial pressure or coercion concerns.

Parenting requirement

Many agencies prefer or require that you are currently parenting at least one child, but this can vary by program. The broader goal is to confirm that you understand pregnancy and birth, have a stable support environment, and are not entering the process without personal parenting experience.

What disqualifies someone — and what doesn't

Things that typically don't disqualify you

  • A prior C-section, in many cases
  • Being single
  • Having a tubal ligation
  • Renting rather than owning your home
  • A past, fully resolved mental health diagnosis, depending on records and current stability

Things that commonly require review or disqualify

  • History of severe preeclampsia, eclampsia, HELLP, or other high-risk complications
  • Active substance use
  • BMI significantly outside the clinic's range
  • Unstable housing or significant unaddressed mental health concerns
  • Prior preterm birth before 36 weeks, depending on details and records

"Requirements exist to protect you and the baby — not to gatekeep for the sake of it. If you're unsure whether you qualify, the only way to know for sure is to ask."

How qualification is actually determined

You start with a preliminary application. If your answers look like a possible fit, the next step is deeper medical, psychological, legal, and records review. Final clearance comes from professionals reviewing your individual history, not from a checklist alone.
