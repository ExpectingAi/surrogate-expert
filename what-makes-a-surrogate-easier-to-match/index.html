Matching

What makes a surrogate easier to match?

Surrogates tend to match faster when they combine strong medical eligibility with realistic compensation expectations, broad matching preferences, responsive communication, workable insurance, and flexibility on intended-parent characteristics.

Short answer: Matchability is not about one “perfect” trait. It is the overlap between what clinics will approve, what intended parents are comfortable with, and what the surrogate expects from the journey.

The factors that usually matter most

FactorHow it can affect matching
Medical historyClear clinic eligibility reduces the risk of a match failing later in screening.
Compensation expectationExpectations that are far above comparable programs can narrow the pool of intended parents.
LocationLegal environment, travel needs, clinic logistics and delivery-state considerations can influence demand.
Prior surrogate experienceSome intended parents value a proven journey, but first-time surrogates can still match quickly.
InsuranceA usable maternity policy can reduce uncertainty and cost for intended parents.
Matching preferencesBroader preferences around family type, geography and communication style create more possible matches.
ResponsivenessFast, clear communication helps agencies and intended parents move a promising match forward.

Compensation matters, but published inventory can be misleading

A list of currently available surrogates is not a clean measure of market compensation. Candidates with higher compensation expectations or narrower preferences may remain visible longer, which can make the visible inventory look more expensive than the compensation level at which matches actually close.

A better way to think about compensation is to compare advertised program ranges, the surrogate's experience and state, and—when available—completed match data rather than relying only on profiles that are still unmatched.

Does experience make matching faster?

Experience can help because intended parents and professionals know the surrogate has already completed the process. But it can also come with higher compensation expectations. A medically strong first-time surrogate with broad preferences and realistic expectations may match faster than an experienced surrogate with a narrower profile.

Can a surrogate improve her chances of matching?

  • Complete the profile fully and accurately.
  • Respond quickly to requests for records and screening.
  • Be clear about compensation and what is negotiable.
  • Keep matching preferences honest but avoid unnecessary restrictions.
  • Make sure pregnancy records and insurance information are available early.
