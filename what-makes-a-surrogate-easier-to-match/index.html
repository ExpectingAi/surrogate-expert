Matching

What makes a surrogate easier to match?

A potential match depends on the overlap between clinic eligibility, intended-parent preferences, the surrogate's preferences, compensation expectations, location, logistics, and communication.

Short answer: There is no single trait that guarantees a fast match. Matchability is the overlap between what clinics will approve, what intended parents are comfortable with, and what the surrogate expects from the journey.

Factors that can affect matching

FactorHow it can affect matching
Medical historyClear clinic eligibility can reduce the risk of a potential match ending during medical screening.
Compensation expectationExpectations above comparable published program ranges may narrow the pool of intended parents.
LocationLegal environment, travel, clinic logistics and delivery-state considerations can affect compatibility.
Prior surrogate experienceSome intended parents prefer an experienced surrogate, while others are comfortable with a first-time surrogate who meets screening requirements.
InsuranceInsurance terms can affect cost and logistics and require professional review.
Matching preferencesPreferences about family type, geography, communication and other journey issues affect how many potential matches are compatible.
ResponsivenessTimely communication and records can help a potential match progress through screening efficiently.

Compensation matters, but published inventory can be misleading

A list of currently available surrogates is not a clean measure of market compensation. Candidates with higher compensation expectations or narrower preferences may remain visible longer, which can make visible inventory look more expensive than compensation levels seen in completed matches.

When comparing compensation, distinguish between published program ranges, a candidate's requested compensation, total package compensation, and—when reliable data is available—compensation in completed matches.

Does experience make matching faster?

Experience may appeal to some intended parents because the surrogate has completed a prior journey, but experience can also come with different compensation expectations. Matching still depends on the full set of medical, legal, logistical and personal compatibility factors.

Can a surrogate improve her chances of matching?

  • Complete the profile fully and accurately.
  • Respond promptly to requests for records and screening.
  • Be clear about compensation expectations and preferences.
  • Keep pregnancy records and insurance information available for professional review.
